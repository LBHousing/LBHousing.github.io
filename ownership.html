Why the density matters

Entities own a small slice of parcels but control a large share of units — they own the multi-unit buildings where most renters live. LLCs average 6.8 units per parcel; limited partnerships average 20.3.

A policy that targets entity-owned property reaches far more tenants per parcel than one targeting individual owners. That's why the Block the Cuts & Build Long Beach Ordinance exempts single-family homeowners earning under $250,000 — it targets institutional-scale ownership, not families.

The pattern: Since 2018, individuals shed 9,309 units while LLCs absorbed 11,148. Because total housing barely grew, most of that LLC gain came from buying up homes that already existed.

Not all trusts are created equal

The vast majority of individual trusts — about 31,007 of them — own a single property. These are homeowners with living trusts for estate planning. LiBRE's analysis does not target these small landlords.

But 139 trusts own 5 or more parcels, controlling an average of 25 units each. They operate at institutional scale yet can receive the same exemptions as a family renting out a spare room.

The gap: A trust owning 25 apartment units can claim the single-family exemption on each property with written notice — so the rent caps and just-cause protections that automatically apply to LLC-owned buildings may not reach those renters.

Concentration, not consolidation

It would be easy to assume a few mega-landlords are swallowing the city. The data says otherwise: the combined share held by the top 10, top 50, and top 200 owners barely moved from 2018 to 2026.

The shift is broad, not concentrated — thousands of individual owners handing buildings to thousands of separate LLCs. That's harder to see than one villain, but it's the real story: 24.8% of the city's housing now sits with multi-property owners operating behind entity structures.

Most entity-owned units (62.5% + 25.4%) are held by owners with just 1–4 parcels. The pattern is decentralized — which is exactly why citywide policy, not case-by-case enforcement, is what reaches it.

That's the case for a community land trust — permanently affordable homes the market can't take back, scaled to the need. That's Build Long Beach.

Renter Protections

What this means for tenant protections.

LLCs, corporations & LPs — 43,300 units

Multi-unit buildings over 15 years old owned by these entities are generally covered by the California Tenant Protection Act. The single-family exemption does not apply to a corporation, REIT, or an LLC with a corporate member. These units likely carry the strongest protections available under current law.

Multi-property trusts — 18,193 units

Individual trusts with 2+ parcels are treated like individual owners, so single-family exemptions can apply. This is the protection gap the Block the Cuts ordinance addresses by targeting institutional-scale owners regardless of entity structure.

Single-property individuals & trusts — the majority of the city

Most Long Beach housing is owned by individuals and single-property trusts — the homeowners and small landlords LiBRE's advocacy explicitly protects. The ordinance exempts single-family homeowners earning under $250,000.

The bottom line: Long Beach is 60% renters. Roughly 44,107 rental units are controlled by multi-property owners operating at scale, often behind opaque structures. Stronger local protections — like the Block the Cuts & Build Long Beach Ordinance — can fill the gaps state law leaves behind.

Know Your Rights

Of the city's 177,588 dwelling units, tens of thousands of renters are covered by the Tenant Protection Act — but many don't know it. Long Beach also has a local Just Cause Termination Ordinance (LBMC §8.99).
