About LiBRE

Housing justice and community power.

LiBRE connects renters with the tools, relationships, and collective strength to protect our homes and build a more equitable future together.

Mission + vision

Housing is a human right.

Long Beach Residents Empowered (LiBRE) is committed to advancing justice in disadvantaged communities through the creation and preservation of affordable housing, renter protections, and community economic development.

Our work is driven by our values of shifting power dynamics so that we can put an end to displacement and homelessness and ensure housing as a human right for everyone who calls Long Beach home.

Our story

From one building to a citywide movement.

LiBRE began in 2016 when local residents came together to resist displacement. What started as building-level organizing has grown into a citywide and regional movement for housing justice.

We are rooted in Long Beach and connected to broader work across the County and nation. Our direction is guided by those most impacted and powered by shared purpose.

New report

Confronting the Long Beach housing crisis.

Long Beach is in the midst of a deepening housing crisis that is displacing longtime residents, eroding community stability, and accelerating racial and economic inequities. Rents are rising far faster than incomes. Homeownership is out of reach for most working families, for recent graduates and for young professionals. Tenant protections remain fragmented and under-enforced. Meanwhile, corporate landlords and speculative investors continue to consolidate control over the city’s housing stock.

These pressures are intensifying in the lead-up to the 2026 World Cup and 2028 Olympics — where Long Beach is set to host several events. In cities that have hosted large-scale events, short-term rental conversions have surged, reducing the availability of long-term housing and fueling displacement in low-income communities.

The report calls for a new civic strategy anchored in two pillars: (1) protecting tenants now, and (2) building a permanently affordable, non-speculative housing ecosystem for the future, drawing on best practices from across California and the nation.
